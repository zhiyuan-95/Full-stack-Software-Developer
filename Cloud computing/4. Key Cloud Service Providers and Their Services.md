# Cloud Market Growth and Major Service Providers

## Summary

The lecture describes rapid growth in public cloud spending and a shift from traditional IT infrastructure toward subscription-based, on-demand services. As adoption increases, the central business question is becoming less about **whether** to use cloud computing and more about **which cloud strategy, services, and providers** best support the organization and its customers.

The lecture then surveys eight major providers in alphabetical order: **Alibaba Cloud, Amazon Web Services, Google Cloud Platform, IBM Cloud, Microsoft Azure, Oracle Cloud, Salesforce, and SAP**.

> **Timing note:** The spending figures and market-position claims below come from the lecture and are time-sensitive. The transcript does not provide its publication date, and these claims have not been independently verified as current.

## Cloud-market forecasts

The lecture cites the following Gartner forecasts:

- Worldwide public-cloud spending would grow by **20.7%**.
- Spending would rise from **$490.3 billion** to **$591.8 billion**.
- Global spending on **Desktop as a Service (DaaS)** would reach **$3.2 billion in 2023**.
- Enterprises would eventually spend more on public-cloud services than on traditional IT solutions.

### Desktop as a Service

**Desktop as a Service** provides subscription-based virtual desktops through the cloud. It allows organizations to move away from traditional client-computing solutions in which desktop software and environments are maintained primarily on local devices.

## Strategic implication

The forecasts suggest that:

- Cloud adoption is increasing rapidly.
- Cloud-provider revenues are growing.
- Organizations are moving toward subscription-based services.
- Public cloud is becoming a larger part of enterprise technology spending.
- Businesses increasingly need a deliberate cloud-adoption strategy.

The relevant question is therefore not merely, “Should we adopt the cloud?” It is, “Which cloud approach will best serve our business and customers?”

# Major Cloud Service Providers

## Provider comparison

| Provider | Primary emphasis in the lecture | Selected offerings |
|---|---|---|
| **Alibaba Cloud** | Broad cloud platform and Alibaba ecosystem | Compute, networking, storage, security, analytics, IoT, application development, migration, and hosting |
| **Amazon Web Services (AWS)** | Extensive infrastructure and platform services | Compute, DevOps, data, analytics, IoT, machine learning, networking, content delivery, robotics, and serverless computing |
| **Google Cloud Platform (GCP)** | Infrastructure, platforms, serverless computing, and Google services | App Engine, computing infrastructure, managed platforms, productivity, communication, collaboration, and storage |
| **IBM Cloud** | Public, private, and hybrid cloud | Bare-metal servers, VMware, virtual private cloud, application modernization, AI, IoT, blockchain, databases, and analytics |
| **Microsoft Azure** | Application development, deployment, and management | Software, platform, and infrastructure services supporting Microsoft and third-party technologies |
| **Oracle Cloud** | SaaS, database services, and enterprise applications | ERP, SCM, HCM, marketing, sales, customer experience, infrastructure, and platform services |
| **Salesforce** | SaaS and customer relationship management | Sales Cloud, Service Cloud, Marketing Cloud, analytics, customer service, and social-channel routing |
| **SAP** | Cloud-based enterprise software | ERP, CRM, HR, finance, and a platform for building or extending business applications |

## Alibaba Cloud

Alibaba Cloud is described as the largest Chinese cloud-computing provider at the time represented by the lecture.

It supports both external customers and the Alibaba Group’s e-commerce ecosystem. Its services include:

- Compute
- Networking
- Storage
- Security
- Monitoring and management
- Communications
- Analytics
- Internet of Things
- Application development
- Data migration
- Web hosting

## Amazon Web Services

**Amazon Web Services (AWS)** was one of the earliest major providers in the cloud-computing market.

AWS supplies infrastructure and platform services to:

- Individuals
- Businesses
- Governments

Its services are generally offered through a **metered, pay-as-you-go model**.

The lecture identifies services in areas such as:

- Compute
- DevOps
- Data and analytics
- Internet of Things
- Machine learning
- Networking
- Content delivery
- Robotics
- Serverless computing

## Google Cloud Platform

**Google Cloud Platform (GCP)** provides:

- Infrastructure services
- Platform services
- Serverless computing environments

Google also uses its cloud infrastructure for products such as **Google Search** and **YouTube**.

The lecture also associates Google Cloud with G Suite products for communication, productivity, collaboration, and storage.

### Google App Engine

**Google App Engine** is a platform for developing and hosting web applications in Google-managed data centers. It can automatically allocate and release resources as application demand changes.

## IBM Cloud

IBM Cloud is presented as a full-stack platform spanning:

- Public cloud
- Private cloud
- Hybrid cloud

Its products and services cover:

- Compute
- Networking
- Storage
- Management
- Security
- DevOps
- Databases

Named offerings and technology areas include:

- Bare-metal servers
- VMware
- Cloud Paks for application modernization
- Virtual private cloud
- Artificial intelligence
- Internet of Things
- Blockchain
- Data and analytics

The lecture says IBM’s acquisition of **Red Hat** strengthened its position in the hybrid-cloud market.

## Microsoft Azure

**Microsoft Azure** is a cloud platform for building, testing, deploying, and managing applications through Microsoft-managed data centers.

Its regional data-center presence provides broad geographic reach while allowing services to be delivered closer to customers.

Azure offers:

- Software services
- Platform services
- Infrastructure services
- Support for Microsoft technologies
- Support for third-party languages, tools, and frameworks

## Oracle Cloud

Oracle Cloud is described as particularly well known for:

- Software as a Service
- Database services
- Enterprise applications
- Data-management capabilities

Its SaaS portfolio includes applications for:

- Enterprise Resource Planning (**ERP**)
- Supply Chain Management (**SCM**)
- Human Capital Management (**HCM**)
- Marketing
- Sales
- Customer Experience (**CX**)

The lecture describes Oracle Data Cloud as a cloud-based data-management platform used to personalize online, offline, and mobile marketing campaigns for targeted audiences.

Oracle also offers cloud infrastructure and platform services.

## Salesforce

Salesforce specializes in **Software as a Service**, particularly **Customer Relationship Management (CRM)**.

Its cloud services include:

- Sales Cloud
- Service Cloud
- Marketing Cloud

These services help organizations:

- Manage relationships with customers, partners, and prospects
- Track analytics in real time
- Support customer-service activities
- Monitor customer interactions across social platforms
- Route issues to appropriate support agents

## SAP

SAP is known for enterprise software delivered through the cloud, including:

- Enterprise Resource Planning
- Customer Relationship Management
- Human Resources
- Finance

The **SAP Cloud Platform** is described as an environment for building and extending business applications through rapid development and innovation cycles in an SAP-managed cloud environment.

## Provider groupings

The providers can be broadly grouped by their emphasis in the lecture:

### Broad infrastructure and platform providers

- Alibaba Cloud
- AWS
- Google Cloud Platform
- IBM Cloud
- Microsoft Azure

These providers offer large collections of infrastructure, platform, development, data, and emerging-technology services.

### Enterprise software and SaaS specialists

- Oracle
- Salesforce
- SAP

These providers are presented as having particular strength in business applications, databases, customer management, and enterprise operations, although some also provide infrastructure and platform services.

## Takeaway

Cloud demand is growing rapidly, but providers differ in their primary strengths. Some offer broad infrastructure and development platforms, while others specialize in enterprise applications, databases, customer management, or hybrid cloud. Organizations should evaluate providers according to their workloads, required service model, existing technology stack, geographic needs, and business objectives—not market popularity alone.
