# Cloud Adoption Strategy: Considerations, Benefits, and Risks

## Summary

Every organization needs a cloud-adoption strategy suited to its workloads, business goals, regulatory obligations, and risk tolerance. Cloud computing can improve agility, flexibility, efficiency, and competitiveness, but migration must be planned carefully to avoid business disruption and problems involving security, compliance, performance, or continuity.

Cloud adoption is not simply a technology decision. It also affects costs, software purchasing, development speed, governance, operations, and business strategy.

## Key adoption considerations

### 1. Infrastructure and workloads

Building and operating private data centers can require significant spending on:

- Servers and other equipment
- Facilities
- Maintenance
- Staffing
- Upgrades and replacement hardware

Cloud computing can reduce initial costs through **pay-as-you-go pricing**, allowing organizations to pay for resources when they use them.

However, **not every workload is ready to move to the cloud as-is**. Organizations should evaluate each workload’s:

- Technical dependencies
- Performance requirements
- Security requirements
- Compliance obligations
- Migration complexity
- Business importance

Some applications may need to be modified before migration, while others may be better kept outside the cloud.

### 2. SaaS and development platforms

Organizations should compare subscribing to applications through **Software as a Service (SaaS)** with purchasing off-the-shelf software.

The comparison should include:

| SaaS approach | Traditional software approach |
|---|---|
| Pay for continued application access | Purchase software licenses |
| Provider typically manages the platform | Organization manages installation and infrastructure |
| Updates may be included in the service | Upgrades may require additional investment |
| Can often be deployed quickly | Deployment may take longer |
| Usually involves recurring operating costs | May require larger upfront costs |

The best option depends on the organization’s financial model, operational needs, and required level of control.

### 3. Speed and productivity

Cloud platforms can reduce the time required to launch applications. A cloud application might be provisioned in hours rather than the weeks or months sometimes required on traditional platforms.

Productivity may also improve through:

- Cloud management dashboards
- Real-time statistics
- Active analytics
- Prebuilt development tools
- Reduced infrastructure administration
- Lower person-hour requirements

## Benefits of cloud adoption

The lecture groups cloud benefits into three categories: **flexibility, efficiency, and strategic value**.

### Flexibility

Cloud computing allows organizations to:

- Scale services up or down according to demand
- Support workloads that fluctuate over time
- Customize applications
- Access services from anywhere with an Internet connection
- Select an appropriate level of control through different as-a-service options
- Combine prebuilt tools and features into solutions that fit specific needs
- Use controls such as encryption and API keys to help protect data

### Efficiency

Cloud services can improve operational efficiency by allowing organizations to:

- Bring applications to market more quickly
- Avoid managing some of the underlying infrastructure
- Access applications and data from almost any Internet-connected device
- Use network backups to reduce the risk of data loss from individual hardware failures
- Avoid purchasing and maintaining as many physical servers
- Pay according to resource usage

These efficiencies allow teams to spend more time on applications and business priorities instead of infrastructure maintenance.

### Strategic value

Cloud providers can give organizations access to current technologies while managing much of the underlying infrastructure.

This can create strategic value by helping organizations:

- Innovate more quickly
- Respond to market changes
- Compete more effectively
- Concentrate on core business priorities
- Experiment without making large hardware investments
- Adopt new technologies faster

## Cloud-adoption risks and challenges

Cloud computing introduces opportunities, but it also creates concerns that business and technology leaders must address.

| Risk area | Main concern |
|---|---|
| **Data security** | Data could be lost, exposed, or temporarily unavailable, causing business disruption. |
| **Governance and sovereignty** | Organizations must understand who controls the data and where it is stored or processed. |
| **Legal and regulatory compliance** | Cloud systems must comply with applicable laws, industry rules, and organizational policies. |
| **Standardization** | Rapidly changing technologies may not integrate or interoperate consistently. |
| **Deployment-model selection** | The organization must choose a deployment model that fits its requirements. |
| **Service-model selection** | The appropriate as-a-service model depends on the desired balance of control and provider responsibility. |
| **Provider selection** | Organizations must evaluate the reliability, security, services, and suitability of potential cloud providers. |
| **Business continuity** | Services must remain available during failures or disruptions. |
| **Disaster recovery** | Organizations need a tested method for restoring applications and data after a serious incident. |
| **Performance** | Cloud services must satisfy the workload’s performance and availability requirements. |

## A practical adoption sequence

The lecture’s considerations suggest the following decision process:

1. Identify the organization’s business objectives.
2. Assess infrastructure costs and operational limitations.
3. Evaluate workloads individually rather than assuming everything should move.
4. Compare SaaS subscriptions with purchasing and maintaining software.
5. Estimate improvements in deployment speed and staff productivity.
6. Choose appropriate deployment and service models.
7. Evaluate cloud service providers.
8. Address security, governance, compliance, and performance requirements.
9. Plan for business continuity, backups, and disaster recovery.
10. Migrate in a way that minimizes business disruption.

## Takeaway

Cloud adoption can provide **flexibility, efficiency, and strategic value**, but it is not a universal one-step solution. A successful strategy matches specific workloads and business goals with suitable cloud models, technologies, services, and providers while actively managing security, compliance, performance, governance, continuity, and disaster-recovery risks.
