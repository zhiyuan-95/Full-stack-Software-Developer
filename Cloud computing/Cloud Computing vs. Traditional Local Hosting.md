# Cloud Computing vs. Traditional Local Hosting

## Summary

Cloud computing is often preferred over local server hosting because it offers greater flexibility, scalability, accessibility, and cost efficiency. Organizations can provision resources quickly, adjust capacity as demand changes, and access applications through a network without purchasing and maintaining all infrastructure themselves.

## NIST definition

The U.S. National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST) defines cloud computing as a model that provides convenient, on-demand network access to a shared pool of configurable computing resources.

These resources include:

- Networks
- Servers
- Storage
- Applications
- Services

They can be rapidly provisioned and released with minimal management effort or interaction with the service provider.

The NIST cloud model contains:

- **Five essential characteristics**
- **Four deployment models**
- **Three service models**

The deployment and service models are not explained in this excerpt.

## Why choose cloud computing?

| Cloud computing | Traditional local hosting |
|---|---|
| Resources can be provisioned quickly | Infrastructure upgrades may be slow and costly |
| Capacity can expand or shrink with demand | Capacity is limited by installed hardware |
| Services can be accessed remotely through a network | Access may be limited to the organization’s local environment |
| Shared infrastructure can reduce costs | The organization pays for and maintains its own equipment |
| Usage can be measured and billed like a utility | Costs often include upfront hardware and ongoing maintenance |
| Much of the infrastructure is managed by a provider | The organization manages the physical infrastructure |

## Five essential characteristics

### 1. On-demand self-service

Users can obtain cloud resources whenever they need them, usually without waiting for manual assistance from the service provider.

The lecture compares this availability to:

- A 24-hour ATM
- A vending machine available at an office or store

Cloud services are generally available every day, including weekends and holidays. However, availability can still be affected by outages or security breaches.

### 2. Broad network access

Cloud resources are accessed through a network.

Public cloud services can generally be used from different locations and devices, including:

- Desktop computers
- Laptops
- Tablets
- Smartphones
- E-readers
- Smart wearables

Public cloud access normally requires an Internet connection. An on-premises private cloud may instead be accessed through an organization’s internal network, or **intranet**, when public access is unnecessary.

### 3. Resource pooling

A cloud provider pools computing resources to serve multiple customers through a **multi-tenant model**.

Resources are dynamically assigned and reassigned according to demand. Customers generally do not need to know or manage the exact physical location of the servers and other resources they use.

Resource pooling gives providers economies of scale, which can make cloud services more cost-efficient for customers.

### 4. Rapid elasticity

Cloud capacity can increase or decrease as demand changes.

Two forms of scaling are:

- **Vertical scaling:** Increasing or decreasing the resources available to a system, such as its processing power or memory.
- **Horizontal scaling:** Adding or removing computing instances to handle changes in workload.

For example, an online retailer can add resources during a holiday sale when customer traffic increases and reduce them after the sale ends.

### 5. Measured service

Cloud usage can be monitored and billed according to the resources used or reserved. This is similar to paying a monthly utility bill and is often described as a **utility billing model**.

Not every cloud-based service charges users directly. Examples may include:

- Free email services such as Gmail, Hotmail, and Yahoo
- Social platforms such as Facebook, Twitter, and WhatsApp
- Free trials or promotional service tiers from AWS, Microsoft Azure, and Google Cloud Platform (GCP)

These services are offered according to each provider’s pricing and access policies.

## Takeaway

Cloud computing replaces much of the fixed capacity and maintenance burden of local infrastructure with shared, network-accessible resources that can be provisioned quickly, scaled with demand, and measured according to usage. Its defining characteristics are **on-demand self-service, broad network access, resource pooling, rapid elasticity, and measured service**.
